# Exact certificate for the F210 obstruction

This file explains the certificate independently of any Gröbner-basis implementation. The checker uses integers, rational coefficients for the preliminary substitution, and arithmetic modulo the prime 101.

## 1. The polynomial system

Write `U_i=25u_i` and `V_j=5v_j`. The twelve integer equations are listed in `data/f210-equations.json` under `original_equations`, in variable order

```
U0,U1,U2,V0,V1,V2,V3,V4,V5,V6.
```

`verify_localization.py` generates the 27 instances of the localization corollary directly from the specified X and Y arrays. Their nonzero polynomial expressions, up to nonzero rational multiples, are precisely these twelve equations. The supports and two vanishing fusion coefficients are checked against `data/f210.json`. The second system is generated independently and agrees under

```
(u0,u1,u2,v0,v1,v2,v3,v4,v5,v6)
   -> (w0,w1,w2,z2,z1,z0,z4,z3,z6,z5).
```

Three linear equations give

```
U0 = (4-7U1-7U2)/5,
V0 = -(5V1+7V3+7V5+1)/5,
V2 = -(5V1+7V4+7V6+1)/5.
```

Substitution and clearing positive denominators leave nine integer polynomials E in the seven variables

```
U1,U2,V1,V3,V4,V5,V6.
```

The checker derives these polynomials from the twelve inputs and compares every coefficient. This is an equivalence over characteristic zero, where division by 5 is valid.

## 2. Exact ideal membership

The file `certificates/f210-basis.txt` contains 28 integer polynomials G. The compressed file `certificates/f210-lift.txt.gz` contains the entries of a 9-by-28 integer polynomial matrix T, serialized row by row, with commas separating entries. The checker verifies, over the integers, all 28 identities

```
G_j = sum_i E_i T_ij.
```

Thus every G_j belongs to the ideal generated by E. This conclusion follows from multiplication and coefficient comparison; no trust in Singular's search algorithm, and no Gröbner-basis assertion, is needed.

After this check, each G_j is divided by the gcd of its coefficients. These gcds are nonzero rational units. The resulting smaller polynomials generate an ideal contained in (E) over the rationals. Their readable forms are also supplied in `certificates/f210-primitive-basis.txt`.

## 3. A finite spanning set

Use degree reverse lexicographic order in the seven-variable order above. The monomials not divisible by any leading monomial of the normalized G are

```
1, V6, V5, V4, V3, V1, U2, U1,
V6^2, V5*V6, V4*V6, V3*V6, V1*V6, U2*V6.
```

The checker obtains this list algorithmically and checks that a pure power of every variable lies in the leading-monomial ideal, ensuring finiteness. Polynomial division shows that these 14 monomials span the quotient by (G), and hence also the quotient by (E). Independence is not required for the argument.

Take two copies of the nine-equation system, with the second copy relabeled according to the permutation above. The 196 pairwise products of spanning monomials span their combined quotient and include the constant 1.

## 4. The coupling is invertible over the rationals

After the rescaling and relabeling, the additional coupling equation is H=0, where

```
H = 5*U0*V0' + 7*U1*V3' + 7*U2*V5' - 5*U0 + 1.
```

A prime marks the second copy, not differentiation. U0 and V0' are replaced using the linear formulas above. Polynomial division by the corresponding copies of G writes multiplication by H on the 196 spanning monomials as a rational matrix M.

Every normalized G has integral coefficients, and the checker verifies that each leading coefficient is nonzero modulo 101. The other denominator is 5. Consequently all reductions take place over the localization of the integers at 101: their rational coefficients have denominators prime to 101. Polynomial division therefore commutes with reduction modulo 101. The checker computes the reduction of M by the same division algorithm over this finite field and verifies

```
det(M) = 85 modulo 101.
```

In particular, det(M) is nonzero over the rationals. Its columns express congruences `H B_j = sum_i M_ij B_i` modulo the two certified ideals. Multiplying these congruences by a solution of `M q=e_1` gives `H sum_j q_j B_j = 1` in the quotient. Thus imposing H=0 forces 1=0. The polynomial system has no solution over any characteristic-zero field extension.

This argument only needs a finite **spanning set** and the exact ideal-membership identities. It does not assume that G is a reduced Gröbner basis, that the quotient is reduced, or that there are exactly 14 geometric solutions. It also does not use the generally invalid inference that modular inconsistency alone implies characteristic-zero inconsistency.

The paper's additional statement that each separate localization has exactly 14 distinct solutions is verified independently, as explained in Section 6 below. The coupling obstruction above does not depend on that stronger statement.

## 5. Independent generation and verification

Singular 4.3.2 produced T using its `liftstd` routine; optional regeneration uses that existing implementation. The large integer coefficients are retained in compressed form so that verification is a short, deterministic arithmetic calculation. On the verification machine the F210 checker took about 4–9 seconds with Python 3.12.14. A separate clean execution with site packages disabled also passed.

## 6. Exactly 14 distinct solutions of a single localization

`certificates/f210-solutions.json` gives a univariate polynomial `p(t)` of degree 14, with `t=V6`, and rational-polynomial expressions in `t` for the other nine original coordinates. `verify_f210_solutions.py` checks that `gcd(p,p')=1` and substitutes all ten coordinates into all twelve original equations, obtaining zero modulo `p`. Each of the 14 distinct complex roots therefore gives a solution; they are distinct because the `V6` coordinate is the root itself.

The 14 spanning monomials already certified in Section 3 bound the number of distinct solutions from above. Hence there are exactly 14. This proof checks the univariate parametrization directly; it does not need an assertion that a particular elimination output is a Gröbner basis. The same conclusion applies to the second localization under its explicit relabeling.
